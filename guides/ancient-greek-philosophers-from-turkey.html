Philosophy · Ionia and beyond

Ancient Greek philosophers from Türkiye, and where to find their cities

Western philosophy began in Ionia, the Greek cities on the Aegean coast of what is now Türkiye. Many of the most famous ancient Greek philosophers were born here, and you can still walk through their cities. This is who they were and where to go.

In short
  • Miletus: Thales, Anaximander and Anaximenes, often called the first philosophers.
  • Ephesus: Heraclitus. Clazomenae: Anaxagoras. Sinop: Diogenes the Cynic.
  • Assos, Soli, Hierapolis: Aristotle's years in Assos, the Stoics Cleanthes and Chrysippus, and Epictetus.
  • Each name links to a page with the main ideas and sources.

The Ionian philosophers of Miletus

Thales (early 6th century BC) is traditionally the first Greek philosopher. He is said to have held that water is the origin of things. Anaximander, his younger fellow citizen, proposed a boundless, indefinite source (the apeiron) and is credited with an early map of the world. Anaximenes chose air. What unites them is the attempt to explain nature by nature, not by myth.

Almost everything we know about them comes from later writers, especially Aristotle. Even the famous story that Thales predicted a solar eclipse, usually dated to 585 BC, comes from Herodotus and is debated.

Visit: Miletus, with its great theatre and the Baths of Faustina.

Heraclitus of Ephesus

Around 500 BC Heraclitus wrote that everything flows and that opposites are bound together by a hidden order, the logos. He was famous for being hard to understand. Visit: Ephesus, and read things to do in Ephesus.

Anaxagoras of Clazomenae

Anaxagoras, from Clazomenae near today's Urla, brought philosophy to Athens in the 5th century BC. He said that Mind (nous) set the world in motion, and that the sun was a fiery stone, which got him prosecuted for impiety. Visit: Clazomenae, with its ancient olive-oil workshop.

Bias of Priene, one of the Seven Sages

Priene, on the slopes of Mount Mykale, was the city of Bias, counted among the Seven Sages of Greece. Visit: Priene, a planned Hellenistic town on terraces.

Aristotle in Assos

Aristotle was born in Stagira in northern Greece, but after Plato's death he spent some years at Assos, at the court of Hermias, around 347 to 345 BC, before moving to Lesbos. Assos was also the birthplace of Cleanthes, the second head of the Stoic school. Visit: Assos.

Diogenes of Sinope

Diogenes the Cynic, who lived in a jar and called himself a citizen of the world, was born in Sinope, today's Sinop on the Black Sea. Visit: Sinop.

The Stoics: Chrysippus and Epictetus

Chrysippus, who gave Stoic logic and ethics their system, came from Soli near today's Mersin. Epictetus, born a slave in Hierapolis, taught that we should care only about what is up to us. Visit: Soli and Hierapolis.

And more

Herodotus, the first historian, was from Halicarnassus (Bodrum). Galen, the physician-philosopher, from Pergamon. Alexander of Aphrodisias, the commentator on Aristotle, from Aphrodisias. The astronomer Hipparchus from Nicaea (İznik) and the geometer Apollonius from Perge. Later, Rumi taught in Konya.

Frequently asked questions

Who was the first Greek philosopher?

By tradition, Thales of Miletus, in the early 6th century BC. Miletus is on the Aegean coast of today's Türkiye, near Didim.

Which Greek philosophers were born in Turkey?

Among others: Thales, Anaximander and Anaximenes (Miletus), Heraclitus (Ephesus), Anaxagoras (Clazomenae), Bias (Priene), Diogenes (Sinope), Cleanthes (Assos), Chrysippus (Soli) and Epictetus (Hierapolis).

Was Aristotle from Turkey?

No. He was born in Stagira in northern Greece, but he lived in Assos, on the coast of today's Türkiye, for some years around 347 to 345 BC.

What was the Ionian school?

The name given to the early philosophers of Ionia, especially Thales, Anaximander and Anaximenes of Miletus, who tried to explain the world through natural causes.
