The 15 best ancient ruins in Türkiye, and the ideas behind them

Türkiye has more Greek and Roman cities than Greece, and some of the oldest monumental sites on earth. This list of the best ancient ruins in Turkey picks fifteen, and adds what most guides leave out: the people who thought and wrote there.

In short
  • For a first trip: Ephesus, Hierapolis-Pamukkale, Aphrodisias and the Göreme valleys.
  • For deep time: Göbekli Tepe and Troy.
  • For drama: Mount Nemrut at sunrise and the theatre of Aspendos.
  • Every place below has its own page with sources, and a hotel suggestion.

The Aegean: the cities of the first philosophers

1. Ephesus

The most complete Roman city in the eastern Mediterranean, with the Library of Celsus, the Great Theatre and the Terrace Houses. Home of Heraclitus. Things to do in Ephesus.

2. Pergamon

An acropolis on a steep hill above Bergama with one of the steepest theatres of antiquity, a great library and the Asklepion healing sanctuary where the physician Galen trained. Pergamon.

3. Aphrodisias

A city of sculptors with a huge, well-preserved stadium and the Sebasteion reliefs. Home of Alexander of Aphrodisias, the great commentator on Aristotle. Aphrodisias.

4. Hierapolis and Pamukkale

A Roman spa city on top of white travertine terraces, with a fine theatre and a museum in the old baths. Birthplace of Epictetus. Hierapolis.

5. Miletus, Priene and Didyma

Three sites within an hour of each other: Miletus, home of Thales and Anaximander; Priene, a planned city on terraces; and the vast Temple of Apollo at Didyma. Miletus, Priene and Didyma.

6. Assos

A temple of Athena on a volcanic hill above the sea, where Aristotle lived for some years. Assos.

7. Troy

Nine cities on one mound, and the setting of Homer's Iliad, with an excellent museum. Visiting Troy.

The south coast: theatres and tombs

8. Aspendos

Probably the best-preserved Roman theatre in the world, still used for performances. Near Antalya. Perge, Aspendos and Side.

9. Perge

Colonnaded streets, a huge stadium and the Hellenistic gate towers. Home of Apollonius, the geometer of the conic sections. Perge.

10. Xanthos, Letoon and Patara

Pillar tombs and the assembly hall of the Lycian League, which Montesquieu praised as a model republic. Lycia.

11. Halicarnassus (Bodrum)

The site of the Mausoleum, one of the Seven Wonders, and the castle museum of underwater archaeology. Birthplace of Herodotus. Bodrum.

Central and eastern Anatolia: deep time

12. Göbekli Tepe

Stone enclosures built by hunter-gatherers about eleven thousand years ago. How to visit Göbekli Tepe.

13. Mount Nemrut

Colossal heads of gods and a king on a mountain top, at their best at sunrise. Mount Nemrut at sunrise.

14. Zeugma

A Roman city on the Euphrates whose mosaics, including the "Gypsy Girl", are in the Zeugma Mosaic Museum in Gaziantep. Zeugma.

15. The rock churches of Cappadocia

Not Greek or Roman, but some of the most beautiful ancient and medieval sites in the country, in the land of the Cappadocian Fathers. Cappadocia in 3 days.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most impressive ancient site in Turkey?

For most visitors, Ephesus: it is the most complete Roman city in the region. For age, Göbekli Tepe; for setting, Mount Nemrut at sunrise.

Does Turkey have more Greek ruins than Greece?

Many guides say so. Western Türkiye, ancient Ionia, Aeolis, Caria and Lycia, was full of Greek cities, and many are well preserved because they were abandoned rather than built over.

Which ruins can you combine in one trip?

Ephesus, Miletus, Priene, Didyma, Aphrodisias and Hierapolis are all within a few hours of each other on the Aegean side, and make a good four- or five-day route.
