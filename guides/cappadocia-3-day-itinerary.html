Cappadocia itinerary: 3 days of rock churches, valleys and balloons

Three days is the right amount of time for Cappadocia: enough for a balloon morning, the painted rock churches, a walk through the valleys and one underground city, without rushing. This Cappadocia itinerary assumes you sleep in Göreme or Ürgüp and have a car or book day tours.

In short
  • Day 1 Göreme Open-Air Museum, Uçhisar, sunset over the Rose Valley.
  • Day 2 Sunrise balloon flight, then Paşabağ, Zelve and Avanos.
  • Day 3 An underground city and the Ihlara Valley walk.
  • Book the balloon for your first morning: flights are often cancelled by wind, so you keep a spare day.

Day 1: Göreme and the painted churches

Start at the Göreme Open-Air Museum, a valley of monastic churches cut into the rock and painted between roughly the 10th and 12th centuries. Pay the extra ticket for the Dark Church, whose frescoes kept their colours because so little light reaches them, and do not miss the Tokalı Church just below the entrance. In the afternoon climb Uçhisar Castle, the highest rock in the area, and end the day on the ridge above the Rose and Red Valleys for sunset.

Philosophy: this was the land of the Cappadocian Fathers, Basil of Caesarea, Gregory of Nazianzus and Gregory of Nyssa, who used Greek philosophy to think about God, the soul and the care of the poor. See our Cappadocia page.

Day 2: A balloon at sunrise, then the fairy chimneys

Hot-air balloons lift off around dawn when the weather allows. Prices change with the season, and flights are cancelled on windy mornings, which is why it pays to book for day 1 or 2 and keep a spare. After breakfast, drive to Paşabağ (Monks' Valley) for the mushroom-capped chimneys, then to the Zelve Open-Air Museum, a village abandoned in the 1950s. Spend the afternoon in Avanos on the Kızılırmak river, the pottery town.

Day 3: Underground city and the Ihlara Valley

Go down into Derinkuyu or Kaymaklı, underground cities with several levels of rooms, stables, chapels and ventilation shafts, used as refuges over many centuries. Then drive to the Ihlara Valley, a canyon with a stream and rock churches along its walls. The walk from Ihlara village to Belisırma is about 4 km. Finish at Selime Monastery, a rock-cut complex at the end of the valley.

Where to stay in Cappadocia

Göreme is at the centre of everything and has the most cave hotels. Uçhisar is quieter, with the best views. Ürgüp is a real town with more restaurants. For the details, read where to stay in Cappadocia.

Add Konya on day 4

Konya, the city of Rumi and the Mevlana Museum, is about two and a half to three hours west by road. It makes a natural fourth day. Our Konya page explains Rumi's thought.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Cappadocia?

Three days is ideal: one for the Göreme Open-Air Museum and valleys, one for the balloon and the fairy chimneys, and one for an underground city and the Ihlara Valley. Two days is possible if you skip Ihlara.

What is the best base in Cappadocia?

Göreme is the most central and has the most cave hotels. Uçhisar is quieter with wide views; Ürgüp is a larger town with more restaurants.

Which underground city is best, Derinkuyu or Kaymaklı?

Derinkuyu goes deeper; Kaymaklı is wider and usually a little less crowded. One is enough for most visitors.

How do you get to Cappadocia?

Fly to Kayseri or Nevşehir (Kapadokya) airport, both about an hour or less from Göreme, or take an overnight bus from Istanbul.
