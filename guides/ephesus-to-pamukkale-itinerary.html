Ephesus to Pamukkale in 4 days: a philosophy itinerary

This route follows Greek philosophy inland, from the city of Heraclitus to the birthplace of Epictetus. It is easiest by car, and each day has one main site, so you have time to stop, read and look.

In short
  • Day 1 Ephesus, night in Selçuk.
  • Day 2 Miletus, night in Didim.
  • Day 3 Aphrodisias, night in Pamukkale.
  • Day 4 Hierapolis and the travertines.
  • Short on time? Skip Miletus and do it in three days.

The route, day by day

Day 1 · Ephesus

Start at the gate when it opens. Walk down Curetes Street to the Library of Celsus, whose façade niches hold copies of statues of Wisdom, Virtue, Thought and Knowledge, then on to the Great Theatre. In the afternoon visit the Ephesus Museum in Selçuk and, at sunset, the lone column of the Temple of Artemis.

Philosophy: Heraclitus and the river that is never the same.

Day 2 · Miletus

About an hour south of Selçuk. Miletus was the home of Thales, Anaximander and Anaximenes, often called the first philosophers. Its great theatre faces what was once the harbour, and the Baths of Faustina still have their statues of a river god and a lion. Priene and the Temple of Apollo at Didyma are close by if you want a fuller day.

Philosophy: the first attempts to explain nature without myth.

Day 3 · Aphrodisias

About two and a half hours from Didim. Aphrodisias had marble quarries nearby and famous sculpture workshops. See the Sebasteion reliefs in the museum, and the stadium, one of the best preserved in the ancient world. Drive on to Pamukkale, about an hour and a half away, for the night.

Philosophy: Alexander of Aphrodisias, the great ancient commentator on Aristotle.

Day 4 · Hierapolis and Pamukkale

Walk up the white travertine terraces early in the morning, barefoot, before the crowds. At the top, Hierapolis has a theatre whose stage reliefs tell the stories of Apollo and Artemis, and a museum inside the Roman baths.

Philosophy: Epictetus, born here as a slave, who taught that some things are up to us and some are not.

Getting around

A rental car gives you the most freedom on this route. Without a car, buses connect Selçuk with Denizli, the city nearest to Pamukkale, and day tours run to most of the sites. Drive times above are approximate.
