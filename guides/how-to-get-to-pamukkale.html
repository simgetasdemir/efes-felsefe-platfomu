Hierapolis · Pamukkale, Denizli

How to get to Pamukkale from Istanbul, Selçuk, Antalya and Cappadocia

Pamukkale, the white terraces with the ancient city of Hierapolis on top, is inland, about 20 km from the city of Denizli. Every route to Pamukkale runs through Denizli, by plane, bus or train, and the last stretch is a short minibus or taxi ride. Here is how to get there from wherever you are.

In short
  • The gateway is Denizli: its bus station and railway station are in the city, its airport (Çardak) about an hour away.
  • From the Denizli bus station, minibuses run to Pamukkale village in about 30 to 40 minutes.
  • From Selçuk (Ephesus) it is about 3 hours by train, bus or car; from Antalya about 4 hours.
  • From Istanbul, fly to Denizli or take an overnight bus.

The last step: Denizli to Pamukkale

Almost every journey ends at the Denizli bus station (otogar), which is next to the railway station. From the lower level, minibuses (dolmuş) leave regularly for Pamukkale village and take about 30 to 40 minutes. Some long-distance bus companies also include a free shuttle from Denizli to Pamukkale: ask when you buy your ticket. A taxi is faster and still affordable for a group.

From Selçuk and Ephesus

This is the most popular way to arrive, as many people pair Ephesus with Pamukkale. Regional trains run from Selçuk to Denizli several times a day and take about three hours, with views of the Meander valley. Buses take a similar time. By car it is about 190 km, roughly three hours, and you can stop at Aphrodisias on the way. See our four-day route from Ephesus to Pamukkale.

From Istanbul

The quickest way is a flight from Istanbul to Denizli Çardak Airport, about an hour and ten minutes. From the airport, shuttle buses and taxis go to Denizli and Pamukkale, about an hour by road. Overnight buses from Istanbul take about ten to twelve hours.

From Antalya

Buses from Antalya to Denizli take about four hours over the Taurus Mountains. By car it is about 240 km. Many hotels in Antalya sell long day tours to Pamukkale, but staying a night is much more relaxed.

From İzmir

Trains from İzmir (Basmane) to Denizli pass through Selçuk and take about four to five hours. Buses are a little faster. İzmir airport has more international flights than Denizli, so it is a good arrival point for a trip that combines Ephesus and Pamukkale.

From Cappadocia

Overnight buses run from Göreme and Nevşehir to Denizli and take about nine to ten hours. If you prefer to fly, connect through Istanbul.

By car, and which gate to use

Pamukkale has three entrances. The town gate, at the foot of the travertines in Pamukkale village, lets you walk up the white terraces barefoot. The north and south gates, on the plateau above, are best if you come by car or taxi and want to start with Hierapolis; both have parking. A good plan is to enter at the top in the morning, walk down through the terraces and leave by the town gate.

How long to stay

One night is enough to see the terraces at sunset and Hierapolis early the next morning, before the day tours arrive. Stay two nights to add Aphrodisias, about an hour and a half away. See where to stay in Pamukkale.

The philosopher of Hierapolis

Hierapolis was the birthplace of Epictetus, the Stoic teacher who grew up a slave and taught that our freedom lies in what is up to us. See our Hierapolis page.

Frequently asked questions

What is the nearest airport to Pamukkale?

Denizli Çardak Airport, about an hour by road, with flights from Istanbul. İzmir airport, about three to four hours away, has more international flights.

How do I get from Denizli to Pamukkale?

By minibus (dolmuş) from the lower level of the Denizli bus station, in about 30 to 40 minutes, or by taxi.

How far is Pamukkale from Ephesus?

About 190 km, or roughly three hours by car, train or bus from Selçuk, the town next to Ephesus.

Can you do Pamukkale as a day trip?

Yes, from Selçuk, Kuşadası or even Antalya, but it means six to eight hours on the road. One night in Pamukkale lets you see the terraces at sunset and early morning, when they are at their best.
