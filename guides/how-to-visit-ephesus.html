How to visit Ephesus: getting there, which gate, tickets and timing

Ephesus is the best preserved Roman city in the eastern Mediterranean, and one of the busiest sites in Türkiye. A little planning changes the visit completely: which gate you use, what time you arrive and whether you buy the extra ticket for the Terrace Houses. Here is how to visit Ephesus well.

In short
  • The ruins are about 3 km from Selçuk, 20 km from Kuşadası and about an hour by car or train from İzmir airport.
  • Start at the upper gate and walk downhill to the Library of Celsus and the Great Theatre.
  • Arrive at opening time: it is cooler and the cruise groups come later in the morning.
  • Allow three hours, or four with the Terrace Houses, which need a separate ticket.

How to get to Ephesus

From Selçuk. Selçuk is the town next to the ruins. A taxi to either gate takes a few minutes, and the lower gate is within walking distance, about 3 km along a tree-lined road. Selçuk is the easiest base, with the Ephesus Museum, the Basilica of St John and the Temple of Artemis in town.

From Kuşadası. Kuşadası is about 30 minutes away by car. Minibuses (dolmuş) run often between Kuşadası and Selçuk, and many taxis and tours wait at the cruise port.

From İzmir and the airport. İzmir Adnan Menderes Airport is about 60 km north of Selçuk. Regional trains run from the airport station and from İzmir to Selçuk, which takes about an hour and a half; by car it is about an hour.

Upper gate or lower gate?

Ephesus has two entrances. The upper gate (south) sits near the State Agora and the Odeon; the lower gate (north) is by the Great Theatre and the old harbour road. The site lies on a slope, so most people start at the upper gate and walk downhill along Curetes Street to the Library of Celsus and out through the lower gate. Taxis wait at both, and drivers know the routine: they drop you at the top and pick you up at the bottom.

Tickets and the Terrace Houses

You can buy tickets at the gate or online from the official museum ticket service of the Turkish Ministry of Culture. The Terrace Houses (Yamaç Evler), the homes of wealthy Roman families with wall paintings and mosaic floors, are under a protective roof halfway down Curetes Street and need a separate ticket. They are worth it: this is where Ephesus feels lived in. If you plan to see many sites in Türkiye, compare the price of the Museum Pass with single tickets. Prices and opening hours change each season, so check them before you go.

When to go

The best months are April to early June and September to October. In July and August the heat is strong by late morning and there is little shade on the marble streets. Whatever the season, go at opening time or in the late afternoon: cruise ship groups usually arrive mid-morning. Bring water, a hat and shoes with grip, since the old paving stones are smooth.

A route through the city

Upper city

From the upper gate, pass the State Agora, the small Odeon where the council met, and the Memmius monument, then turn down Curetes Street.

Curetes Street

Look for the Temple of Hadrian with its carved arch, the public latrines and the entrance to the Terrace Houses.

The Library of Celsus

At the bottom stands the façade of the Library of Celsus, built in the 2nd century AD. Its niches hold copies of statues of Wisdom, Virtue, Thought and Knowledge.

The Great Theatre

Walk along Marble Street to the Great Theatre, which held about 25,000 people, then out by the Harbour Street towards the lower gate.

After the ruins

In Selçuk, the Ephesus Museum holds the statues of Artemis found in the city. On the hill, the Basilica of St John has views over the plain, and below it stands the single column of the Temple of Artemis, once one of the Seven Wonders. The hillside village of Şirince is a good place for dinner.

The philosopher of Ephesus

Heraclitus lived in Ephesus around 500 BC. He taught that everything flows, and that the world is held together by a hidden order he called the logos. Read more on our Ephesus page.

Frequently asked questions

How long do you need at Ephesus?

About three hours for the ancient city, four with the Terrace Houses. Add half a day for the Ephesus Museum, the Basilica of St John and the Temple of Artemis in Selçuk.

Which entrance is best at Ephesus?

Most visitors start at the upper (south) gate and walk downhill to the Library of Celsus and the Great Theatre, leaving by the lower (north) gate. Taxis wait at both gates.

Are the Terrace Houses worth it?

Yes, for most visitors. They are the best preserved Roman houses in the city, with wall paintings and mosaics, and they are under a roof, which is welcome on a hot day. They need a separate ticket.

Can you visit Ephesus without a tour?

Yes. The site is well signposted and easy to visit on your own from Selçuk or Kuşadası. A guide or audio guide helps you understand what you are seeing.

What is the best time to visit Ephesus?

Spring and autumn, at opening time or in the late afternoon. Summer middays are very hot, and cruise groups usually arrive mid-morning.
