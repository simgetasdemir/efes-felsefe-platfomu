Göbekli Tepe · Şanlıurfa

How to visit Göbekli Tepe from Şanlıurfa: tickets, tours and what to see

Göbekli Tepe is a hilltop of great stone enclosures built by hunter-gatherers some eleven thousand years ago, thousands of years before writing or the wheel. It is easy to visit from the city of Şanlıurfa. Here is how to visit Göbekli Tepe, and what to see around it.

In short
  • Göbekli Tepe is about 15 km northeast of Şanlıurfa, roughly 20 to 30 minutes by car or taxi.
  • UNESCO dates its use to around 9600 to 8200 BC. It is a World Heritage Site since 2018.
  • See the Şanlıurfa Archaeology Museum too: it holds many of the finds.
  • Stay in Şanlıurfa and add Karahan Tepe and Harran.

What Göbekli Tepe is

The site is a series of round and oval enclosures ringed by T-shaped limestone pillars, some over five metres tall. Many are carved with foxes, boars, snakes, vultures and scorpions, and some have arms and hands, as if the pillars were people. It was built by people who did not yet farm, which surprised archaeologists: monumental building had been thought to come after agriculture.

What the enclosures were for is still debated. They were first described as temples; more recent work has found evidence of daily life nearby, and some researchers now see them as large communal buildings. Our Göbekli Tepe page sets out what is known and what is not.

How to get to Göbekli Tepe

The site is about 15 km northeast of the Şanlıurfa city centre. The simplest way is a taxi or a rental car. There are also shuttle services from the visitor centre and organised tours from Şanlıurfa, some of which include Karahan Tepe and Harran. Şanlıurfa has an airport (GAP) with flights from Istanbul and Ankara.

What to see at the site

From the visitor centre a short walk or shuttle takes you up to the main excavation, covered by a large membrane roof. Walkways circle the enclosures so you can look down on the pillars. Look for the fox carved on the pillars of Enclosure D and for the hands on the central pillars. Allow about one to two hours.

Karahan Tepe and the Taş Tepeler

Göbekli Tepe is not alone. It belongs to a group of related sites in the hills around Şanlıurfa, known as the Taş Tepeler ("Stone Hills"). The best known, Karahan Tepe, has a room of pillars carved out of the bedrock and a striking carved human head. It is about an hour from the city.

The Şanlıurfa Archaeology Museum and Balıklıgöl

Back in the city, the Şanlıurfa Archaeology Museum is one of the largest in Türkiye and shows finds from Göbekli Tepe and from the region, including the "Urfa Man", a life-size stone figure dated to the same period. Next door, the Haleplibahçe Mosaic Museum protects Roman villa mosaics. In the evening walk to Balıklıgöl, the pools of sacred carp linked to the story of Abraham.

Harran

About 45 km south of the city, Harran has beehive-shaped mud-brick houses and the ruins of a medieval mosque and university. It was a centre of learning in late antiquity and the early Islamic period.

Frequently asked questions

How old is Göbekli Tepe?

UNESCO dates the site to around 9600 to 8200 BC, in the Pre-Pottery Neolithic. That makes it one of the oldest known monumental sites in the world.

How far is Göbekli Tepe from Şanlıurfa?

About 15 km northeast of the city centre, usually 20 to 30 minutes by car or taxi.

How long do you need at Göbekli Tepe?

One to two hours at the site. Add half a day for the Şanlıurfa Archaeology Museum and Balıklıgöl, and a further half day for Karahan Tepe or Harran.

Is Göbekli Tepe a temple?

That is debated. It was first described as a temple; newer research suggests the enclosures may also have been communal buildings used by people who lived nearby.
