Lycia and the Lycian Way: Xanthos, Letoon, Patara and where to stay

The cities of Lycia governed themselves as a league with a common council, in which large cities had three votes, middling ones two and small towns one. Montesquieu called it the model of an excellent confederate republic, and Alexander Hamilton quoted him in the Federalist Papers. Today the same coast is walked on the Lycian Way.

In short
  • Kalkan is the closest small town to Patara and Xanthos, with sea-view hotels.
  • Kaş, a little further east, is livelier and a good base for diving, boat trips and the coastal path.
  • Xanthos and the Letoon are a UNESCO World Heritage site; Patara has the league's assembly hall and a long beach.

Kalkan: closest to the ruins

Kalkan is a former fishing village of white houses above a small harbour. Patara and Xanthos are both a short drive away, so you can be at the sites early and on the beach in the afternoon.

Kaş: by the sea

Kaş has a small ancient theatre facing the sea, many restaurants and boat trips along the coast. It suits a longer stay and a day or two on the Lycian Way.

Xanthos and the Letoon

Xanthos was the capital of Lycia. UNESCO calls its rock tombs, pillar tombs and pillar-mounted sarcophagi a novel type of funerary architecture, and notes that the Nereid Monument found here directly influenced the Mausoleum of Halicarnassus. The longest and most important texts in the Lycian language were found here and at the nearby Letoon, the league's sanctuary.

Patara

Patara held the assembly hall of the Lycian League, built in the early first century BC. Nearby stand a Roman lighthouse and one of the longest beaches in Türkiye. Saint Nicholas was born here, according to tradition, in the third century.

The Lycian Way

The Lycian Way is a long-distance walking route along the coast, established in 1999, from near Fethiye towards Antalya. You do not need to walk all of it: a single coastal stretch between Kalkan and Kaş is a good taste of the path.

The model republic

Were I to give a model of an excellent confederate republic, I should pitch upon that of Lycia.

Montesquieu, The Spirit of the Laws, Book IX, 3 (Nugent translation). Quoted by Alexander Hamilton in Federalist No. 9.
