Mount Nemrut at sunrise: how to visit the stone heads

On top of Mount Nemrut, over 2,100 metres up, a king of the first century BC built himself a burial mound and surrounded it with giant statues of gods and of himself. Their heads now sit on the ground, and they turn gold at dawn. Seeing the Mount Nemrut sunrise takes some planning. Here is how.

In short
  • Sunrise is seen from the east terrace, sunset from the west terrace.
  • The summit road is generally open from spring to autumn; check before you go in winter.
  • Most people stay in Kahta or the village of Karadut and leave before dawn.
  • Bring warm clothes even in summer: it is cold on the top before sunrise.

What you will see

Mount Nemrut (Nemrut Dağı) was the tomb-sanctuary of Antiochus I, king of Commagene in the first century BC. On the peak is an artificial mound of loose stones. On its east and west terraces stood rows of seated colossal statues: the king, Greek and Persian gods merged into one (Zeus-Oromasdes, Apollo-Mithras-Helios-Hermes, Heracles-Artagnes-Ares) and the goddess of Commagene. Earthquakes toppled the heads, which now stand in a row at the foot of the bodies. The site has been on the UNESCO World Heritage List since 1987.

The king also left a long inscription explaining what he had built and why. Our Mount Nemrut page explains how this mixing of Greek and Persian gods fits into the history of ideas.

Sunrise or sunset on Mount Nemrut?

Both are beautiful. Sunrise is the classic: you climb in the dark, wait on the east terrace, and watch the first light hit the faces. Sunset on the west terrace is easier, warmer and has fewer early starts. If you can, stay a night nearby and do both.

From the car park it is a walk of about 600 metres, steep in places, up to the terraces. Allow 20 to 30 minutes.

How to get to Mount Nemrut

The usual bases are Kahta (in Adıyaman province) and the small village of Karadut, which is closer to the top. Tours also leave from Şanlıurfa, Malatya and Cappadocia, but those are long nights. The nearest airport is Adıyaman. In winter, snow can close the road to the summit, so the usual season is roughly May to October.

On the way: Arsameia, Cendere Bridge and Karakuş

Many tours stop at Arsameia, the old Commagene capital, where a relief shows the king shaking hands with Heracles; at the Roman Cendere Bridge; and at Karakuş, a burial mound for the royal women, with a column topped by an eagle.

Frequently asked questions

What time is sunrise on Mount Nemrut?

It depends on the season: roughly 5 am in early summer and 6 to 7 am in autumn. Tours from Kahta usually leave two to three hours before sunrise.

Is it cold on Mount Nemrut?

Yes. The summit is over 2,100 metres high and the wind is strong before dawn. Bring a warm jacket, even in July.

When is Mount Nemrut open?

The summit road is usually open from about May to October. In winter, snow can close it; check locally before you go.

Who built Mount Nemrut?

Antiochus I, king of Commagene, in the first century BC, as his tomb-sanctuary. The burial chamber itself has never been found.
