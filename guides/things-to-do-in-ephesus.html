Ephesus · Selçuk, İzmir

12 things to do in Ephesus and Selçuk (beyond the Library of Celsus)

Most visitors give Ephesus three hours and leave. That is a pity, because the ruins are only half of it. Here are the best things to do in Ephesus and in Selçuk, the small town next to it, in the order that makes a good day or two.

In short
  • Arrive when the gates open and walk downhill from the upper gate.
  • Pay the extra ticket for the Terrace Houses: they are the highlight for many visitors.
  • Keep the afternoon for Selçuk: the Ephesus Museum, the Basilica of St John and the Temple of Artemis.
  • Sleep in Selçuk or Şirince so you can start early.

Things to do inside ancient Ephesus

1. Walk Curetes Street from the upper gate

Start at the upper (Magnesian) gate and walk downhill. Curetes Street runs past fountains, honorific monuments and the Temple of Hadrian, with its carved arch, to the lower city. Walking downhill saves your legs and gives you the best view of the Library of Celsus as it comes into sight.

2. See the Library of Celsus

The two-storey façade of the Library of Celsus, built in the 2nd century AD as a library and a tomb for a Roman governor, is the image everyone knows. Look for the four niches with statues of Wisdom, Virtue, Thought and Knowledge (the ones on site are copies).

3. Step inside the Terrace Houses

Under a modern roof on the slope above Curetes Street are the homes of wealthy Roman families, with wall paintings, mosaic floors and marble panels still being pieced together. It is a separate ticket, and worth it.

4. Sit in the Great Theatre

The Great Theatre held about 25,000 people. It is where, according to the Acts of the Apostles, the silversmiths rioted against Paul. From the top rows you can see the Harbour Street running towards what was once the sea.

5. Stand where Heraclitus lived

Ephesus was the home of Heraclitus, around 500 BC, the philosopher of change and the logos. Nothing of his house survives, but reading a few of his fragments in the city makes the visit different. Our Ephesus page has his key ideas, with sources.

Things to do in Selçuk, next to Ephesus

6. The Ephesus Museum

In the centre of Selçuk, the museum holds the famous statues of Artemis of Ephesus found in the city, and finds from the Terrace Houses. Visit it after the ruins, when you know what you are looking at.

7. The Temple of Artemis

One of the Seven Wonders of the ancient world is now a single re-erected column in a marshy field, often with a stork's nest on top. It is a quiet, strange and moving place at sunset.

8. The Basilica of St John and Ayasuluk Hill

On the hill above the town are the ruins of the great basilica built over the traditional tomb of John the Apostle, and a Byzantine and Turkish fortress. The views over the plain are wide.

9. The İsa Bey Mosque

At the foot of the hill stands a 14th-century mosque of the Aydınoğulları, built partly with reused ancient columns: a good place to see how one civilisation builds on another.

10. The House of the Virgin Mary

On Bülbül Mountain above Ephesus is a small chapel venerated by Christians and Muslims as the last home of Mary. It is a pilgrimage site, so dress modestly.

Around Ephesus

11. Şirince village

A hillside village of stone houses about 8 km above Selçuk, known for fruit wines, olive oil and lunch in a garden.

12. Day trips to Priene, Miletus and Didyma

Within about an hour to the south are three more great sites: Priene on its terraces, Miletus, the city of the first philosophers, and the Temple of Apollo at Didyma. See our Priene and Didyma and Miletus pages.

Frequently asked questions

How long do you need in Ephesus?

Allow at least three hours for the ancient city, and four with the Terrace Houses. Add half a day for the museum, the Temple of Artemis and the Basilica of St John in Selçuk.

What is the best time to visit Ephesus?

Spring (April to early June) and autumn (September and October). In any season, go as soon as the gates open: by late morning the site is hot and busy with tour groups.

Are the Terrace Houses worth it?

Yes, for most visitors. They are the best-preserved Roman houses in the region, with paintings and mosaics, and they are less crowded than the main street.

Where should I stay for Ephesus?

Selçuk is the closest town, about 3 km from the ruins. Şirince is a quiet village above it, and Kuşadası is a beach resort about 20 km away. See where to stay for Ephesus.
