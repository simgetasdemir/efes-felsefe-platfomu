Troy to Pamukkale in 7 days: an ancient sites road trip in Türkiye

The Aegean coast of Türkiye is lined with ancient cities, and they follow each other neatly from north to south. This seven-day route visits them by car, from Homer's Troy to the white terraces of Pamukkale, with one or two main sites a day and a night in a different town each evening.

In short
  • Day 1 Troy, night in Çanakkale.
  • Day 2 Assos, night in Behramkale.
  • Day 3 Pergamon, night in Bergama.
  • Day 4 Selçuk, and Day 5 Ephesus, nights in Selçuk.
  • Day 6 Aphrodisias, and Day 7 Pamukkale, nights in Pamukkale.
  • Start from Istanbul or Çanakkale; finish at Denizli airport or continue to Antalya.

Before you go

This route is easiest with a rental car: the drives are two to three hours a day on good roads, and several sites are outside towns. You can pick up a car in Istanbul or Çanakkale and drop it at Denizli airport. Without a car, buses link all the towns, but plan an extra day or two. The best seasons are spring and autumn; in summer, visit the sites early and drive in the afternoon.

The route, day by day

Day 1 · Troy

From Istanbul the drive to Çanakkale takes about four to five hours, over the 1915 Çanakkale Bridge. Troy is 30 km south of the town. Walk round the mound, where at least nine cities were built on top of each other, then see the excellent Troy Museum by the entrance.

Ideas: Homer, and Plato's quarrel with the poets.

Day 2 · Assos

About an hour and a half south. The Temple of Athena sits on a volcanic hilltop above the sea, facing the island of Lesbos, and the village of Behramkale climbs up to it. Below, the old harbour has stone hotels and fish restaurants.

Ideas: Aristotle lived here for some years around 347 to 345 BC; Cleanthes, head of the Stoic school, was born here.

Day 3 · Pergamon

About two hours to Bergama. Take the cable car up to the Acropolis, with the steepest theatre of the ancient world and the foundations of the Great Altar. Below the town, the Asklepieion was a healing sanctuary where patients slept in the hope of a curing dream.

Ideas: Galen, the physician-philosopher, was born in Pergamon. The city's great library rivalled Alexandria's.

Day 4 · Selçuk

About two and a half hours south. Arrive for the afternoon: see the Ephesus Museum, climb to the Basilica of St John, and watch the sunset by the lone column of the Temple of Artemis. Have dinner in the hillside village of Şirince.

Day 5 · Ephesus

Be at the upper gate at opening time and walk down Curetes Street to the Library of Celsus and the Great Theatre. Buy the extra ticket for the Terrace Houses. In the afternoon, rest, or drive 45 minutes to Priene, a planned Hellenistic town on the slopes of Mount Mykale.

Ideas: Heraclitus and the river that is never the same.

Day 6 · Aphrodisias

About two hours inland. Aphrodisias was a city of sculptors: see the reliefs of the Sebasteion in the museum, the Tetrapylon gate and one of the best preserved stadiums of the ancient world. Drive on to Pamukkale, about an hour and a half, and walk onto the travertines at sunset.

Ideas: Alexander of Aphrodisias, the great ancient commentator on Aristotle.

Day 7 · Hierapolis and Pamukkale

Go early, before the day tours. Walk barefoot over the white terraces, then explore Hierapolis above them: the theatre, the long necropolis and the museum in the Roman baths. Swim in the Antique Pool if you like. In the evening, fly home from Denizli or continue to Antalya.

Ideas: Epictetus, born here a slave, who taught that some things are up to us and some are not.

Shorter or longer

Five days: skip Assos and Pergamon, start in İzmir and do days 4 to 7. Ten days: add a day for Miletus and Didyma, the cities of the first philosophers (see Ephesus to Pamukkale in 4 days), and begin with two days in Istanbul (see Turkey in 10 days).

Frequently asked questions

Is 7 days enough to see the ancient sites of Turkey?

Seven days is enough for the western coast: Troy, Assos, Pergamon, Ephesus, Aphrodisias and Pamukkale. Sites further east, such as Cappadocia, Mount Nemrut and Göbekli Tepe, need a separate trip or a flight.

Do I need a car for this route?

A car makes it much easier, as Troy, Assos, the Asklepieion and Aphrodisias are outside the towns. Without one, buses connect Çanakkale, Bergama, Selçuk and Denizli, but you will need more time and some taxis.

Where should I start and finish?

Start in Istanbul or Çanakkale and finish at Denizli, which has flights back to Istanbul. You can also do the route in reverse, or start in İzmir for a shorter version.

Which is the best ancient site on this route?

Ephesus is the most complete city, but many visitors are most moved by Aphrodisias, which is quieter, or by the theatre of Pergamon on its steep hillside.
