Visiting Troy in Türkiye: the ruins, the museum and Çanakkale

The ruins of Troy are on a low mound called Hisarlık, near the Dardanelles. What you find there is not one city but many, built on top of each other for over four thousand years. Visiting Troy in Türkiye is easy from Çanakkale. Here is what to see, and how to make sense of it.

In short
  • Troy is about 30 km south of Çanakkale, roughly 30 to 40 minutes by car or bus.
  • See the Troy Museum as well as the ruins: it explains the layers.
  • Allow two hours for the site and one to two for the museum.
  • Combine with Assos to the south and the Gallipoli battlefields across the strait.

Why Troy is many cities

Archaeologists count at least nine main settlements at Troy, from the early Bronze Age to the Roman city of Ilion. Each was built on the ruins of the one before, so a walk around the mound is a walk through time. Which layer, if any, matches the Troy of Homer's Iliad is still debated; many scholars point to the Late Bronze Age levels known as Troy VI and VIIa. The site has been on the UNESCO World Heritage List since 1998.

What to see on the site

A marked path takes you round the mound. Highlights are the sloping walls and towers of Troy VI, the ramp of Troy II, the deep trench dug by Heinrich Schliemann in the 1870s, which cut through several layers, and the small Roman theatre (odeon) and council house. Near the entrance stands a modern wooden horse that children can climb.

The Troy Museum

The Troy Museum, opened in 2018 near the entrance to the site, is one of the best archaeology museums in the country. It shows finds from Troy and from the region of the Troad, and makes the layers of the city easy to follow. Do not skip it.

Troy and the poets

For the ancient Greeks Troy was the setting of the Iliad, the first great poem of their tradition, and Homer was often called the teacher of Greece. Plato, in the Republic, argued about whether poets like Homer should be allowed in the ideal city. Our Troy page tells the story, with sources.

Getting there and where to stay

Çanakkale is the natural base, with hotels on the waterfront and a lively student town. Buses and minibuses run to Troy, and many tours include it. Çanakkale is about five to six hours by road from Istanbul, and there is a small airport.

Combine Troy with Assos and Gallipoli

Assos, where Aristotle lived for some years, is about an hour and a half south and makes a beautiful stop on the way down the coast. Across the Dardanelles are the Gallipoli battlefields of 1915. See Assos and Aristotle.

Frequently asked questions

Is Troy worth visiting?

Yes, especially if you visit the Troy Museum as well. The ruins are less spectacular than Ephesus, but the layers of so many cities on one hill, and the link to Homer, make it unique.

How far is Troy from Istanbul?

About five to six hours by road to Çanakkale, then 30 to 40 minutes to the site. Day tours from Istanbul exist but are long; one night in Çanakkale is better.

Was the Trojan War real?

No one knows for sure. Troy was a real, large Bronze Age city that was destroyed more than once; whether a war like the one in the Iliad happened is still debated.

How long do you need at Troy?

About two hours on the site and one to two hours in the Troy Museum.
