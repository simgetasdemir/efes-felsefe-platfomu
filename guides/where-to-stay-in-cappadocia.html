Where to stay in Cappadocia: Göreme, Uçhisar or Ürgüp

In the 4th century three thinkers from this region, the Cappadocian Fathers, brought Greek philosophy into Christian thought. Centuries later monks painted the rock churches of Göreme. Where you stay decides how easily you reach the valleys at sunrise.

In short
  • Göreme is the most central: the Open Air Museum is a short walk away and most balloon flights pick up here.
  • Uçhisar, on the highest point, is quieter and has wide views over the valleys.
  • Ürgüp is a real town with more restaurants and a slightly slower pace.

Göreme: in the middle of it all

Göreme sits among the fairy chimneys, the cones left by the erosion of volcanic tuff. UNESCO describes its rock-cut churches as unique evidence of Byzantine art after the period of Iconoclasm. Many hotels here are cave rooms cut into the rock.

Uçhisar and Ürgüp

Uçhisar, a few kilometres away, climbs up to its rock castle and has the widest views. Ürgüp is a larger town with wineries and restaurants, about fifteen minutes by car from Göreme.

What to see

Start at the Göreme Open Air Museum early in the morning. Walk one of the valleys in the afternoon light and visit an underground city such as Derinkuyu or Kaymaklı, multi-storey refuges cut into the rock.

The Cappadocian Fathers

Basil of Caesarea, his brother Gregory of Nyssa and their friend Gregory of Nazianzus were all trained in Greek philosophy. Basil urged the young to take what was useful from pagan poets and philosophers. Gregory of Nyssa argued that God cannot be fully grasped and that growth toward the good never ends.

See the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on Gregory of Nyssa.

On to Konya

Konya, the city of Rumi, is about three hours away by road. A night there completes our three-day Cappadocia and Konya route.
