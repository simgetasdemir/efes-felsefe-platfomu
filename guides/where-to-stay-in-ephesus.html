Ephesus · Selçuk, İzmir

Where to stay for Ephesus: Selçuk, Şirince or Kuşadası

Ephesus is the city of Heraclitus, the philosopher who said you cannot step into the same river twice. To see it at its best you want to be at the gate early, before the heat and the tour buses. That makes where you sleep the first decision of the trip.

In short
  • Selçuk is the practical choice: the ruins are about 3 km away, and the town has the Ephesus Museum, the Basilica of St John and the last column of the Temple of Artemis.
  • Şirince, a hillside village about 8 km above Selçuk, is the quiet, romantic choice with stone houses and local wine.
  • Kuşadası, about 20 km away on the coast, has the most hotels, beaches and restaurants, and the cruise port.

Selçuk: closest to the ruins

Selçuk is a small, easy town at the foot of the Ayasuluk hill. From here a short taxi ride, or a long walk, takes you to the ancient city, so you can be there when the gates open. In the evening you can walk to the Basilica of St John on the hill and to the single standing column of the Temple of Artemis, once one of the Seven Wonders. The Ephesus Museum, with the statues of Artemis found in the city, is in the town centre.

Selçuk also has a railway station with regional trains from İzmir, which makes it the easiest base if you are not driving.

Şirince: a village in the hills

Şirince is a former Greek village of whitewashed stone houses in the hills above Selçuk, known for its fruit wines and olive oil. It is quieter and greener, and many guesthouses are restored village homes. You will need a short drive or a taxi down to Ephesus each morning.

Kuşadası: by the sea

Kuşadası is a busy resort town on the coast, about half an hour from Ephesus by car. Choose it if you want beaches, a wide choice of hotels and restaurants, or if you are combining Ephesus with a cruise. Many day tours to Ephesus start from here.

How to see Ephesus

The site has two entrances, an upper and a lower gate. Many visitors start at the upper gate and walk downhill along Curetes Street to the Library of Celsus and the Great Theatre, which held about 25,000 people. The Terrace Houses, the homes of wealthy Roman families with their wall paintings and mosaic floors, are under a protective roof and need a separate ticket. Go early in the morning, bring water and a hat, and allow at least three hours.

The philosopher of Ephesus

Heraclitus lived here around 500 BC. Only fragments of his book survive, quoted by later writers. He taught that everything is in flux, held together by an underlying order he called the logos.

Upon those who step into the same rivers, different and again different waters flow.

Heraclitus, fragment DK B12. See the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on Heraclitus for the debate about what he meant.
