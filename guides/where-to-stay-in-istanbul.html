Istanbul · Byzantium

Where to stay in Istanbul for Hagia Sophia and Byzantine history

For a thousand years Constantinople was the capital of Byzantium, and its scholars copied and taught the Greek philosophers. Most of the Plato and Aristotle we read today reached us through Byzantine manuscripts. If you come for that history, stay close to the old peninsula.

In short
  • Sultanahmet puts Hagia Sophia, the Archaeology Museums and the Basilica Cistern within a short walk.
  • Sirkeci, just downhill, is a little cheaper and close to the tram, the ferries and Marmaray.
  • Karaköy, across the Galata Bridge, is livelier in the evening and one tram stop from the old city.

Sultanahmet: under the dome

Sultanahmet is the heart of the historic peninsula on the UNESCO World Heritage list. From here you can walk to Hagia Sophia, dedicated in 537, early in the morning before the queues. The historian Procopius wrote that its dome seemed not to rest on masonry but to hang from heaven. The Istanbul Archaeology Museums, a few minutes away in the grounds of Topkapı, hold the painted sarcophagi of Clazomenae, another stop on our map.

Sirkeci and Karaköy

Sirkeci sits between Sultanahmet and the Golden Horn and has good transport links. Karaköy, across the Galata Bridge, has cafés, galleries and views back to the old city. Both make sense if you want more choice or a lower price.

Chora and the land walls

Give a second day to the Chora (Kariye) with its 14th-century mosaics and frescoes, then walk a stretch of the Theodosian land walls. The Süleymaniye Mosque, named with Hagia Sophia in UNESCO's listing, is a good place to end the day.

On to İznik

İznik, ancient Nicaea, is about two to two and a half hours away. It was the birthplace of the astronomer Hipparchus, the meeting place of two great church councils in 325 and 787, and in the 16th century the centre of Ottoman tile making. One night there is enough.
