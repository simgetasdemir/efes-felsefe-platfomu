Where to stay in Pamukkale: village, Karahayıt or Denizli

Pamukkale is best early in the morning and late in the afternoon, when the white terraces glow and the day trippers have gone. Staying nearby is what makes that possible.

In short
  • Pamukkale village sits at the foot of the travertines: walk to the entrance, and see the terraces at sunrise.
  • Karahayıt, a few kilometres north, has larger hotels with thermal pools.
  • Denizli, the nearby city, is the transport hub, with the bus station and a wider choice of hotels.

Pamukkale village

The small village below the terraces is mostly guesthouses and family hotels, many with a view of the white slopes. From here you can walk up the travertines from the lower entrance as soon as the site opens, and come back down for sunset.

Karahayıt

Karahayıt is a spa village just north of Hierapolis, known for its reddish, iron-rich thermal springs. It has bigger resort hotels with thermal pools, which make a good rest day on a longer trip.

Denizli

Denizli is the city in the valley below, about half an hour away. It has the main bus station, city hotels and restaurants, and is a practical stop if you arrive late or travel on by bus.

What to see

Walk the travertines barefoot, as shoes are not allowed on the terraces. At the top, the ancient city of Hierapolis has a large theatre with carved stage reliefs, a long necropolis, and an archaeology museum inside the Roman baths. The Antique Pool, with its warm water over fallen columns, is a separate ticket. Aphrodisias, with its sculpture and stadium, is about an hour and a half away and makes an excellent day trip.

The philosopher of Hierapolis

Epictetus was born in Hierapolis in the first century AD and grew up as a slave in Rome. Freed, he became one of the most widely read Stoic teachers. His pupil Arrian wrote down his lessons in the Discourses and the short Handbook.

Some things are up to us and some are not up to us.

Epictetus, Handbook 1. See the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on Epictetus.
