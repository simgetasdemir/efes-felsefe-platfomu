Ani

Kars · 10th–13th c.

Grigor Magistros (c. 990–1058), educated in Ani, capital of the Bagratid Armenian kingdom, translated Plato’s Timaeus and Phaedo into Armenian. Shirak, Ani’s region, was also the homeland of Anania Shirakatsi, counted the father of the exact sciences in Armenia. The city carries Byzantine, Seljuk and Georgian layers; it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Who: Grigor Magistros; the tradition of Anania of Shirak

Key ideas

  1. Plato in Armenian

    Grigor Magistros was educated in Ani in both religious and secular learning, and learned Syriac and Greek. He translated Plato’s Timaeus and Phaedo into Armenian; the translations are lost, but his letters preserve a medieval intellectual’s thoughts on philosophy and politics. Wikipedia ↗

  2. Gathering knowledge

    Grigor collected every Armenian manuscript of scientific or philosophical value he could find: the works of Anania Shirakatsi, and translations from Callimachus, Andronicus of Rhodes and Olympiodorus. A culture builds its own heritage of thought by bringing its books together. Wikipedia ↗

  3. The mathematician of Shirak

    In the 7th century Anania Shirakatsi studied with Tychicus in Trebizond, then came home to write books on mathematics, astronomy and geography: tables of the four operations, recreational puzzles and the first geography in Armenian. Knowledge travelled from the Black Sea to the Caucasus with one teacher. Wikipedia ↗

  4. A city of layers

    The Seljuks took Ani in 1064; the cathedral served for a time as a mosque and the Mosque of Manuchihr rose beside the gorge; then came the Georgians and again Armenian merchants. Ani shows how the same stones can house different faiths. UNESCO ↗

A walk on site

  1. The Cathedral of Ani Completed in 1001 by the architect Trdat, known for its pointed arches.
  2. The Church of Tigran Honents Dated 1215, its interior covered in frescoes.
  3. The Mosque of Manuchihr One of the oldest Turkish mosques in Anatolia, above the Arpaçay gorge.

Listen

Ani stood on the edge of the deep Arpaçay gorge, a capital once called the city of a thousand and one churches. A prince who grew up here, Grigor, learned Greek and translated Plato into his own language. He brought the Phaedo, on the immortality of the soul, and the Timaeus, on a craftsman god ordering the cosmos, to Armenian readers. Those translations are lost; but once a thought enters a language, it leaves a trace there. Stand beneath the broken dome of the cathedral and listen to the wind. Then ask: when a city falls, what remains, the stones or the ideas?

What we don't know

Grigor’s Plato translations are lost; how much he translated, and from what text, we know only from his letters and later reports. Little is certain of Anania’s life beyond what he wrote himself.

Legacy

Grigor’s letters are an important source for medieval Armenian thought, and Anania is counted the father of the exact and natural sciences in Armenia. The pointed arches of Ani Cathedral still come up in debates on the origins of Gothic architecture.
