Assos

Ayvacık, Çanakkale · 4th–3rd c. BC

After Plato's death (347 BC) Aristotle came to Assos and stayed about three years. Cleanthes, second head of the Stoic school, was also from Assos.

Key ideas

  1. Starting from appearances

    Aristotle begins inquiry from how things appear and from reputable opinions, then works through the puzzles they raise. Appearances are generally reliable but can be revised when science requires. SEP §3 ↗

  2. Founder of logic

    He built the first systematic theory of deduction, the syllogistic. He treated logic not as a science but as the tool (Organon) of all the sciences. SEP §4 ↗

  3. Three kinds of science

    Theoretical sciences (metaphysics, mathematics, physics) aim at knowing, practical sciences (ethics, politics) at living well, productive sciences (crafts, rhetoric, poetics) at making. SEP §2 ↗

  4. Cleanthes: agreement with nature

    Cleanthes of Assos added “with nature” to Zeno’s formula “living in agreement”. The Stoic goal thus became living in agreement with nature. SEP §4 ↗

Listen

When Plato died in 347 BC, Aristotle left the Academy in Athens and came to Assos. He stayed about three years, then crossed to the island of Lesbos opposite, where he studied living things. Another son of Assos, Cleanthes, became the second head of the Stoic school. The story goes that he carried water at night to earn his living and listened to philosophy by day. Look out towards Lesbos from the Temple of Athena: this is the view Aristotle saw.

Timeline

  1. 384 BC

    Aristotle born in Stagira; joined Plato’s Academy at 17.

  2. 347 BC

    After Plato’s death came to Assos and stayed about three years; then two years on Lesbos with Theophrastus.

  3. 343 BC

    Went to Pella to tutor the young Alexander.

  4. 335 BC

    Returned to Athens and founded the Lyceum.

  5. 3rd c. BC

    Cleanthes of Assos succeeded Zeno as head of the Stoa.

What we don't know

How long he tutored Alexander is unclear. Whether his surviving works are as he wrote them or were later stitched together by editors is also disputed.

Legacy

According to SEP, only Plato rivals him in philosophical influence.

Connections

  • Soli and Tarsus: Chrysippus was the pupil of Cleanthes of Assos and his successor at the Stoa.
  • Ephesus: The Stoics took fire and logos from Heraclitus; Cleanthes wrote interpretations of him.
  • Sinope: Cynicism fed into the Stoa through Crates and Zeno; Cleanthes grew up in this line.
  • Aphrodisias: Alexander became the most influential ancient commentator on Aristotle.
  • Pergamon: Galen brought Aristotle’s logic and study of nature into medicine.
