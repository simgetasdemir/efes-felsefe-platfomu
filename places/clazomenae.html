Clazomenae

Urla, İzmir · 500–428 BC

The first philosopher to bring philosophy to Athens. He argued that a "nous", a mind, set the cosmos in order.

Who: Anaxagoras

Key ideas

  1. No coming to be

    Following Parmenides, he held that nothing truly comes to be or perishes. Coming to be is mixing; perishing is separation. SEP §3.1 ↗

  2. A share of everything in everything

    Even the smallest piece of matter contains a share of every basic kind. Growth is the separating out of what is already there. SEP §3.3 ↗

  3. Predominance

    What a thing is depends on the largest share in it. Gold is gold because it holds more gold than anything else. SEP §3.4 ↗

  4. Nous and the vortex

    Mind (nous) starts a vortex whose rotation separates matter into the world we see. Whether nous is divine is disputed. SEP §4 ↗

Listen

Anaxagoras was born in Clazomenae around 500 BC and moved to Athens; he is counted as the first philosopher to bring philosophy to that city. He held that there is a share of everything in everything, and that the order of the cosmos was set in motion by nous, mind. In Athens he was charged with impiety for saying that the sun was not a god but a burning stone, and he had to leave the city.

Think

Is the order of the world the work of a mind, or does it arise by itself?

Timeline

  1. c. 500 BC

    Born in Clazomenae to a wealthy family; gave up his inheritance for philosophy.

  2. 20–30 years

    Lived in Athens as a friend of Pericles. First Greek to explain eclipses by one body screening another.

  3. 428 BC

    Charged with impiety for calling the sun a red-hot stone; went to Lampsacus and died there. The city honoured him with a children’s festival.

What we don't know

The dates of his arrival in Athens, of his book and of his trial are all disputed. Only 22 fragments of his work survive.

Legacy

His idea of nous shaped Plato’s Timaeus and Aristotle’s theory of mind.
