Edirne, the Ottoman capital before Istanbul, turned two ideas into stone. In the hospital of Bayezid II, opened in 1488, patients were treated with music and the sound of water too, and a medical school stood beside it. Mimar Sinan’s Selimiye is the idea of a space gathered under one dome; it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Who: Mimar Sinan; the hospital of Bayezid II

Key ideas

  1. Medicine of body and soul

    Since Avicenna, Islamic medicine treated body and soul together. The hospital of Bayezid II put this into practice: Evliya Çelebi describes musicians playing for the patients several times a week, and the belief that different musical modes eased different ills. Wikipedia ↗

  2. Knowledge and care together

    Beside the hospital stood a medical school: medicine was taught at the bedside. This joining of knowledge and practice belongs to a tradition that runs in Anatolia from the Asclepieion of Pergamon onward. Wikipedia ↗

  3. One dome, one space

    At the Selimiye Sinan set a great dome on eight piers and opened the interior without dividing it: everyone who enters stands beneath the same dome, inside one whole. UNESCO describes it, “with its single great dome and four slender minarets”, as the culmination of Sinan’s work. UNESCO ↗

  4. A contest with Hagia Sophia

    The Tezkiretü’l-Bünyan, which records Sinan’s own account of his works, says Sinan counted the Selimiye his masterpiece and claimed its dome left Hagia Sophia’s behind; in fact it is only about half a metre wider. A master in dialogue with the greatest work of the past: a lesson in how tradition and innovation meet. Wikipedia ↗

This section is about no single philosopher but the thought that became architecture and medicine in Edirne: healing body and soul together, and the unity of space.

Listen

Edirne grew where the Tunca and Maritsa rivers meet. The Roman emperor Hadrian gave it his name, and the Ottomans made it their capital for more than eighty years. In the Complex of Bayezid II on the edge of town there was a hospital. In the middle of its hexagonal hall a pool gave the sound of water, and music played beneath the dome for the patients. Physicians treating the body tried not to forget the soul. Across town, at the end of his life, Sinan raised a dome under which everyone who enters stands in the same light. In the middle of the Selimiye, look up and ask: what can a space teach a person?

Think

Apart from medicine, what has truly healed you?

Timeline

  1. 2nd c. AD

    Emperor Hadrian gives the city his name: Hadrianopolis.

  2. 1369

    The Ottomans make it their capital.

  3. 1432

    Mehmed II is born in Edirne.

  4. 1453

    The capital moves to Istanbul; Edirne remains a second capital.

  5. 1488

    The complex and hospital of Bayezid II open.

  6. 1568–1575

    Sinan builds the Selimiye.

  7. 1997

    The hospital becomes Trakya University’s Health Museum; Council of Europe Museum Prize in 2004.

  8. 2011

    The Selimiye is inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

What we don't know

We know how music therapy worked in the hospital mostly from Evliya Çelebi’s account; details of which mode was played for which illness may have been mixed with later writers.

Legacy

The Selimiye is the most studied work of Sinan and of Ottoman architecture. The hospital is today Türkiye’s only hospital museum, and a historical example in current debates on healing with music.
