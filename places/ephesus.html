Ephesus

Selçuk, İzmir · 6th–5th c. BC

The philosophy of change and logos was born here. Heraclitus held that everything flows, but that the flow follows a measure, a "logos".

Key ideas

  1. Flux

    Some things stay the same only by changing: a river is a river because its water is always renewed. According to SEP, the reading “you cannot step into the same river twice” comes from Plato and misses Heraclitus’ point. SEP §3.1 ↗

  2. Unity of opposites

    Waking and sleeping, youth and old age are connected states that turn into one another. This is not a logical mistake but opposites passing into each other over time. SEP §3.2 ↗

  3. Fire kindled in measures

    No god or human made the world order; it is an everliving fire, kindled and quenched in measures. Fire turns into sea and sea into earth in fixed proportions. SEP §4 ↗

  4. Logos and knowledge

    Most people fail to see the logos that governs all things. Heraclitus wrote in riddles on purpose, to teach us to read the world like an oracle. SEP §5 ↗

Listen

Heraclitus lived in Ephesus around 500 BC. All that survives of his book are fragments quoted by later writers. In them the world never stands still: the river flows, fire burns, day turns into night. Yet for Heraclitus this change is not random; everything happens according to a measure, what he called the logos. In Ephesus you can see it for yourself: this harbour city once stood on the sea, and the silt carried by the river left it on dry land.

Think

Are you the same person you were ten years ago? What stays unchanged and makes you you?

Timeline

  1. c. 500 BC

    Active in Ephesus, then under Persian rule.

  2. —

    Wrote a single book, said to have been deposited in the Temple of Artemis.

  3. Later

    The Stoics adopted his physics of fire.

What we don't know

Most anecdotes about his life seem invented to illustrate his character. Only one of the river fragments (B12) is securely his, and the structure of his book is disputed.

Legacy

Since Plato he has been read as the philosopher of universal flux, and since Hegel as a process philosopher.
