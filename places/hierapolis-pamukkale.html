Key ideas

  1. Reason and assent

    Humans are rational beings who examine their impressions and give or withhold assent. What we come to believe is governed by consistency with what we already believe. SEP §4.1 ↗

  2. Volition (prohairesis)

    The true self is volition, and by nature it cannot be hindered. Body, possessions, reputation and other people are “externals” outside it. SEP §4.3 ↗

  3. Value and indifference

    Only a good state of volition, virtue, is truly good. Externals are indifferent in value, yet should still be used with care. SEP §4.4 ↗

  4. Correcting emotions

    Grief, fear and envy spring from false judgements about where happiness lies. Correct the judgement and you correct the emotion. SEP §4.5 ↗

Listen

Epictetus was born a slave in Hierapolis in the 50s AD. He was freed in Rome and began to teach Stoic philosophy. When the emperor Domitian banished philosophers from Rome, he went to Nicopolis in Greece and founded his school there. He wrote nothing himself; his student Arrian took notes of his lessons. The heart of his teaching is simple: tell apart what is up to us from what is not. We cannot choose what happens to us, only our judgements about it.

Think

Think of something that upset you today. How much of it was up to you, and how much was not?

Timeline

  1. 50s

    Born in Hierapolis; a slave of Epaphroditus, an official at Nero’s court.

  2. —

    Studied with the Stoic Musonius Rufus, later freed.

  3. After 89

    When Domitian banished philosophers from Italy, he founded a school at Nicopolis.

  4. c. 135

    Died at Nicopolis.

What we don't know

The Discourses are usually credited to his student Arrian, but a few scholars argue Epictetus wrote them himself.

Legacy

Marcus Aurelius was deeply impressed by him. The Enchiridion was translated into Latin in 1497 and widely read for two centuries.
