İznik

Bursa · 2nd c. BC – 16th c. AD

Hipparchus, a founder of astronomy, was born in ancient Nicaea. The city later hosted two great church councils and, in the 16th century, the famous İznik tiles.

Listen

Hipparchus was born here, in ancient Nicaea, around 190 BC. He discovered the precession of the equinoxes, calculated the length of the year to within minutes and compiled a catalogue of about 850 stars. He is thought to have made the first known table of chords, which is why some historians say trigonometry began with him. A thousand years later the city hosted two great councils: the first in 325, the second in 787 in Hagia Sophia. In the 16th century İznik tiles adorned Ottoman mosques.

What we don't know

Only one of Hipparchus' works survives, his commentary on Aratus and Eudoxus; we know the rest through later writers such as Ptolemy.

Legacy

Ptolemy's Almagest was built largely on Hipparchus' observations and methods.
