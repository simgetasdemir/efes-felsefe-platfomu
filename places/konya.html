Konya

Konya · 13th c.

Rumi was born in Balkh in 1207 and died in Konya in 1273. After his death his disciples organised the Mevlevi order; Konya adds Sufi thought to the map.

Who: Jalāl al-Dīn Rūmī

Key ideas

  1. The limits of reason

    Rumi likens philosophers who rely on reason alone to people walking on “wooden stilts”. SEP reads this not as a rejection of reason but as a critique close to al-Ghazali’s. SEP §5 ↗

  2. Truth known by living it

    He resists knowing truth only by correspondence, authority or reason. Truth has a side that can only be known through experience. SEP §5 ↗

  3. Against blind imitation

    He criticises unquestioning obedience to jurists and finds a purely authority-based view of truth wanting. SEP §5 ↗

  4. Universal and particular intellect

    Universal intellect concerns the divine, particular intellect this world. Human intellects also differ in their origin. SEP §5 ↗

SEP has no separate entry on Rumi; his ideas draw on the Rumi section of “Mysticism in Arabic and Islamic Philosophy”, his life on Britannica.

Listen

Jalal al-Din Rumi was born in 1207 in Balkh, in today’s Afghanistan. His family moved west and finally settled in Konya, the Seljuk capital. In 1244 his meeting with the wandering dervish Shams of Tabriz changed his life. His great work, the Masnavi, opens with the lament of a reed cut from its reed bed and made into a flute. Rumi died in Konya in 1273; his disciples organised the Mevlevi order after him.

What we don't know

He is said to have travelled to Syria once or twice and perhaps met Ibn Arabi, but this is uncertain. That he composed his poems in ecstasy is the chroniclers’ belief.

Legacy

Some call the Masnavi “the Persian Qur’an”. By the end of the 20th century his poetry had become a global phenomenon.
