Lycia: Xanthos and Patara

Antalya, Muğla · 4th c. BC – Roman period

The cities of Lycia formed a league with a common council: large cities had three votes, middling ones two, small towns one. Montesquieu chose Lycia as the model of an excellent confederate republic; the American Federalist Papers cited it too.

Who: The Lycian League; Montesquieu and the Federalist Papers

Key ideas

  1. Proportional representation

    For Montesquieu, Lycia was a league of twenty-three towns: in the common council large ones had three votes, middling ones two, small ones one. Montesquieu IX.3 ↗

  2. Shared justice and government

    According to Hamilton, the common council appointed the judges and magistrates of the cities. Federalist No. 9 ↗

  3. Federal institutions

    According to Britannica, Lycia enjoyed relative freedom under Persia and Rome and kept its federal institutions until Augustus. Britannica ↗

  4. Its own language and art

    According to UNESCO, the longest and most important texts in the Lycian language were found at Xanthos-Letoon. UNESCO ↗

Art and monuments

  • Pillar tombsIn UNESCO's words, rock tombs, pillar tombs and pillar-mounted sarcophagi are a novel type of funerary architecture.
  • Nereid MonumentAccording to UNESCO, this monument at Xanthos directly influenced the Mausoleum of Halicarnassus.
  • Patara assembly hallThe council house of the Lycian League, built in the early 1st century BC.

A walk on site

  1. Xanthos Pillar tombs in the Lycian capital.
  2. Letoon The league's federal sanctuary, where the trilingual inscription was found.
  3. Patara The league's assembly hall, a Roman lighthouse and a long beach.

Listen

The cities of Lycia lived for centuries as a league. In the common council, large cities had three votes, middling ones two and small towns one; the council also appointed judges and magistrates. In the 18th century Montesquieu, in The Spirit of the Laws, held up Lycia as the model of an excellent confederate republic. In 1787 Alexander Hamilton quoted him in Federalist No. 9 and brought Lycia into the debate over the American constitution. Sit on the steps of the assembly hall at Patara: here small cities looked for a way to decide together with large ones without being crushed by them.

Think

In a shared decision, how can the large and the small be fairly represented?

What we don't know

The Lycian language is not fully understood, and sources differ on when the league was first formed.

Legacy

Through Montesquieu and the Federalist Papers, the Lycian League lived on as a model in modern debates on federalism.
