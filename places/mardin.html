Mardin and Nusaybin

Mardin · 5th–7th c.

The School of Nisibis (Nusaybin) was one of the centres that translated Greek works into Syriac. The Arabs came to know Greek thought chiefly through these Syriac translations.

Who: The School of Nisibis: Narsai

Listen

Soon after 471, Narsai and his companions left Edessa, today's Urfa, for Nisibis, today's Nusaybin, and founded a school. Its teachers wrote on literature, history and theology and translated from Greek into Syriac. In Britannica's words, it was primarily through Syriac translations that the Arabs became acquainted with Greek thought; Aristotle's logical works also took this road. The monasteries of Deyrulzafaran near Mardin and Mor Gabriel in Midyat are living homes of this Syriac tradition today.

What we don't know

Little detail survives on how Aristotle was taught at the school; exactly how this translation chain connects to the Arabic translations of Baghdad is still being studied.

Legacy

The Syriac translations were the first link in the road that carried Greek philosophy into Arabic and from there to Latin Europe.
