Miletus

Didim, Aydın · 6th c. BC

The first school of Western philosophy. Thales looked for the first stuff of the world; Anaximander called the source of all things the "apeiron", the boundless.

Who: Thales, Anaximander, Anaximenes

Key ideas

  1. Explaining nature from within

    The Milesians explained events through the workings of nature itself, not through the intervention of gods. SEP sees this as a radical break with earlier accounts. SEP §2 ↗

  2. Thales and water

    According to Aristotle, Thales took water as the first principle, perhaps because nourishment and growth depend on it. SEP notes that Aristotle had no first-hand text by Thales. SEP §2 ↗

  3. Anaximander and the apeiron

    The source of all things is not a familiar element but the indefinite, the boundless. Opposites such as hot and cold separate from it and “pay penalty” to each other in the order of time. SEP §2 ↗

  4. Anaximenes and air

    Rarefied air becomes fire; condensed, it becomes wind, cloud, water, earth and stone. One process explains all the variety. SEP §2 ↗

Listen

Miletus was once one of the richest harbour cities of the Aegean. In the 6th century BC three thinkers here tried to explain the world not through stories of the gods but through nature itself. According to Aristotle, Thales took water to be the principle of all things. Anaximander called the source of everything the apeiron, the boundless. Anaximenes looked for it in air. Their answers may sound strange today, but their question is still ours: is there one principle beneath all this variety?

Think

Do you think there is one principle beneath everything, or is the world truly made of many?

Timeline

  1. Early 6th c. BC

    Thales lived in Miletus; he is said to have written no book.

  2. 6th c. BC

    Anaximander, said to have drawn the first map of the world.

  3. 6th c. BC

    Anaximenes, the last of the three Milesians.

What we don't know

SEP judges the story that Thales predicted the eclipse of 585 BC almost certainly wrong. The tradition that Anaximander was Thales’ pupil is also uncertain.

Legacy

The Presocratics laid the foundation of the philosophy that Plato and Aristotle developed.
