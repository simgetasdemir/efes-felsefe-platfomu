Mount Nemrut

Adıyaman · 1st c. BC

King Antiochus I (r. 69–34 BC) built a hierotheseion on the summit: a temple-tomb and house of the gods. Colossal statues that merge Greek and Iranian gods bring the beliefs of two worlds together in stone.

Who: King Antiochus I of Commagene

Key ideas

  1. House of the gods

    Antiochus' monument is a hierotheseion: at once temple, tomb and house of the gods. UNESCO ↗

  2. Gods with two names

    According to UNESCO, Zeus was merged with Oromasdes, the Iranian Ahuramazda, and Heracles with Artagnes. UNESCO ↗

  3. Identity in stone

    Inscriptions identify the statues: the king presents himself as heir to both Greek and Iranian traditions. UNESCO ↗

Nemrut is a stop for art and belief; instead of a philosopher, a king's attempt to join two worlds.

Art and monuments

  • Colossal statuesOn the east and west terraces, five giant seated limestone statues identified by their inscriptions as deities; their heads have fallen.
  • Lion and eagleGuardian animal statues at each end of the rows.
  • The tumulusAn artificial summit of crushed stone, 50 m high and 145 m across.

A walk on site

  1. East terrace The colossal statues and fallen heads at sunrise.

  3. Arsameia and the Cendere Bridge Commagene's summer capital and a Roman bridge.

Listen

Commagene was a small kingdom, but it stood between two great worlds: Greece to the west, Iran to the east. King Antiochus I ruled from 69 to 34 BC and built a temple-tomb for himself on the mountain's summit. The giant figures seated on the terraces are gods, and each bears two names: Zeus is merged with Oromasdes, the Iranian Ahuramazda; Heracles with Artagnes. Climb to the east terrace at sunrise. Among the fallen heads you will see two cultures trying to speak a single language.

Think

Does merging two beliefs or ideas enrich them, or does it take something from both?

What we don't know

The king's burial chamber beneath the tumulus has never been found.

Legacy

Nemrut is seen as one of the most striking monuments where the religious worlds of East and West are shown side by side.
