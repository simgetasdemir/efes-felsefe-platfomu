Pergamon

Bergama, İzmir · 2nd c. AD

Home to one of the greatest libraries of the ancient world, and birthplace of Galen (129–c. 200), physician and philosopher.

Who: Galen

Key ideas

  1. Demonstration

    The highest knowledge comes from demonstration built on premises taken from the essence of the subject. Galen ranked premises in four grades: scientific, dialectical, rhetorical and sophistical. SEP §3.1 ↗

  2. Experience and reason together

    He rejected medical schools that relied on experience alone. For him, observation gains meaning within a framework built by reason. SEP §3.2–3.3 ↗

  3. A three-part soul

    Reason sits in the brain, spirit in the heart, desire in the liver. He tried to show the brain’s role through a spinal cord experiment on a pig. SEP §3.1 ↗

  4. Every part has a purpose

    In “On the Function of the Parts” he argued that every detail of the body serves a purpose. SEP §5 ↗

Listen

The library founded by the kings of Pergamon rivalled the one in Alexandria. Galen was born in this city in 129 AD. As a young man he studied with teachers from different philosophical schools, then became a physician and treated gladiators. Later he became physician to emperors in Rome. For Galen a good doctor had to know logic, study nature and live ethically; in other words, he also had to be a philosopher.

Think

Can the health of the body be separated from the health of the soul?

Timeline

  1. 129

    Born in Pergamon, son of an architect; began philosophy at 14 and medicine at 16.

  2. After 149

    Studied in Smyrna, Corinth and Alexandria.

  3. 157

    Returned to Pergamon as physician to the gladiators.

  4. 160s

    Settled in Rome; became court physician to Marcus Aurelius.

  5. 192

    A fire destroyed much of his personal library.

What we don't know

His traditional date of death is around 200, but some sources say he lived more than ten years longer. Most of his philosophical works survive only in fragments.

Legacy

His medicine and philosophy were hugely influential through the Middle Ages, in Europe and in the Arabic-Islamic world.
