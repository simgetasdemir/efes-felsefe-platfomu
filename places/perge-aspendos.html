Key ideas

  1. Three curves from one cone

    Apollonius studied the ellipse, parabola and hyperbola as different sections of a single cone. MacTutor ↗

  2. The names

    We still take the names parabola, ellipse and hyperbola from their use in the Conics. MacTutor ↗

  3. Books saved by translation

    Of eight books, the first four survive in Greek and the next three only in Arabic; the eighth is lost. MacTutor ↗

Listen

Apollonius of Perga was born here around 262 BC and worked in Alexandria. In his Conics he studied the curves you get by cutting a cone at different angles, and gave them the names we still use: ellipse, parabola, hyperbola. In his own time he was called "the Great Geometer". As you walk Perge's colonnaded street, think how these curves still shape our world, from planetary orbits to satellite dishes. Then go to the theatre at Aspendos, one of the best preserved of antiquity, and test its acoustics.

What we don't know

As MacTutor stresses, little is known of Apollonius' life; most dates are approximate.

Legacy

The Conics prepared the ground for Kepler's description of planetary orbits as ellipses; today the curves are everywhere from lenses to satellite dishes.
