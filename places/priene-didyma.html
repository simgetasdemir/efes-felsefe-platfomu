Priene, the city of Bias, one of the Seven Sages, was refounded on its present site around 350 BC on a grid plan. Nearby Didyma held Apollo's oracle; there the Milesians began a temple meant to be the largest in the Greek world, and never finished it.

Who: Bias of Priene; the oracle of Didyma

Key ideas

  1. The Seven Sages

    Ancient tradition counts Bias among the Seven Sages, alongside Thales; many short maxims are attributed to him. Wikipedia ↗

  2. A planned city

    Priene was laid out on an even grid of six main streets crossed at right angles by fifteen. Britannica ↗

  3. Oracle and reason

    Didyma was Apollo's oracle: while the first philosophers of Miletus tried to explain nature by reason, people at the neighbouring temple put their questions to the god. Britannica ↗

Art and monuments

  • Temple of Athena PoliasAccording to Britannica, built by Pythius, probable architect of the Mausoleum; dedicated by Alexander in 334 BC.

Listen

Bias was one of the Seven Sages of ancient tradition; Plato lists him alongside Thales. His city, Priene, first stood at the mouth of the Maeander; around 350 BC its people moved it to its present site at the foot of a mountain and laid it out on a grid of streets at right angles. Alexander the Great dedicated its main temple in 334 BC; the dedication inscription is now in the British Museum. Nearby Didyma held the oracle of Apollo. There the Milesians began a temple meant to be the largest in the Greek world, but never finished it. As you walk among its unfinished columns, ask: is it beginning a work that matters, or finishing it?

Think

Would you begin a work you knew you could never finish?

What we don't know

Bias' life is known mostly from later writers' anecdotes; how many of the sayings attributed to him are really his is uncertain.

Legacy

Priene's grid is among the best preserved examples of Greek town planning; the unfinished temple at Didyma was one of the largest building projects of antiquity.
