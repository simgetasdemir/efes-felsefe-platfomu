Diogenes (c. 404–323 BC), emblem of Cynic philosophy, was a citizen of Sinope who fled or was exiled from the city.

Key ideas

  1. Nature against convention

    What is not shameful in private cannot be shameful in public. For him, what lacks reason is convention, not nature. IEP §2 ↗

  2. Reason as guide

    Without reason, a person needs a halter like an animal. His contentiousness served reason and virtue, not mere negation. IEP §2 ↗

  3. Refuting by showing

    He is said to have answered an argument against motion by walking. He mocked Plato’s definition of a human as a “featherless biped” with a plucked chicken. IEP §2 ↗

  4. Training (askēsis)

    Living in a jar showed the simple life as a form of training. His aim was to set courage against fortune, nature against convention, reason against passion. IEP §1–2 ↗

Listen

Diogenes was born in Sinope around 404 BC. He fled or was exiled from the city and went to Athens. There, the stories say, he lived in a large jar and walked about in daylight with a lamp, saying he was looking for a human being. He mocked the habits of society in order to question them, and argued that only a life in accordance with nature is free. Most of these stories are anecdotes collected centuries later by Diogenes Laertius.

Timeline

  1. c. 404 BC

    Born in Sinope.

  2. —

    Exiled over the debasing of coinage; coin finds support the story.

  3. Athens

    Met Antisthenes; lived in a jar in the Metroön.

  4. c. 323 BC

    Died; the stories of his death are treated as legend.

What we don't know

Whether he wrote anything is disputed. The story that pirates captured him and sold him as a slave in Corinth may be invented.

Legacy

In antiquity he became the model of the Cynic sage.
