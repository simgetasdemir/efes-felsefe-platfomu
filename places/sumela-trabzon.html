Trabzon and Sumela

Trabzon · 7th–15th c.

Born in Trebizond, Bessarion (1403–1472) became the Renaissance thinker who tried to reconcile Plato and Aristotle; his library is the core of the Biblioteca Marciana in Venice. The Empire of Trebizond was a centre of Byzantine science, and the Sumela Monastery, clinging to its cliff, is an emblem of withdrawal into contemplation.

Who: Bessarion of Trebizond; the scholars of Trebizond

Key ideas

  1. Plato or Aristotle?

    In the fifteenth century Byzantium and Italy argued over whether Plato or Aristotle stood higher. Bessarion answered George of Trebizond’s attack on Plato with In calumniatorem Platonis: he defended Plato as the philosopher closest to Christian thought, yet tried to reconcile the two without belittling Aristotle. SEP §2.3 ↗

  2. Giving away a library

    After the fall of Constantinople Bessarion made it his task to save Greek manuscripts, and in 1468 he gave the hundreds he had gathered to Venice. The gift became the core of the Biblioteca Marciana; it was largely in this way that Greek thought reached Renaissance Europe. SEP §1 ↗

  3. Plethon’s pupil

    At Mystras Bessarion studied Neoplatonism, mathematics and astronomy with Gemistos Plethon. As a cardinal he carried on the interest in Plato that his teacher had stirred in Florence. SEP §1.3 ↗

  4. Astronomy from Tabriz to Trebizond

    Late in the thirteenth century Gregory Chioniades, backed by the emperor of Trebizond, went to the Ilkhanid capital Tabriz, learned Persian and Arabic astronomy and translated it into Greek; around 1315 he was living in Trebizond. Science here crossed a bridge between East and West. Wikipedia ↗

  2. The rock church and its frescoes Frescoes covering the inner and outer walls of the cave church.
  3. Hagia Sophia of Trabzon Built in the 13th century under the Empire of Trebizond, famous for its frescoes.

Listen

You reach Sumela by climbing through the forest of the Altındere valley. The monastery clings so closely to the cliff that from afar it looks like part of the rock. For centuries monks withdrew here from the world to pray, read and cover the walls with paintings. Trebizond below was the opposite: a crowded port where the Silk Road came down to the Black Sea. Bessarion, born in that city, became both a monk and a man of the world: he defended Plato, rescued manuscripts and gave his books to a city. Looking down into the valley from the monastery, ask: does thinking need withdrawal from the world, or a life within it?

Think

To think, do you need to step away from the world, or into the crowd?

Timeline

    Bessarion is born in Trebizond.

  6. 1439

    At the Council of Florence he argues for church union; made a cardinal.

  7. 1461

    Mehmed II takes Trebizond.

  8. 1468

    Bessarion gives his library to Venice.

  9. 1472

    Dies in Ravenna.

What we don't know

Sources give Bessarion’s birth year as 1389, 1395 or 1403. The founding of Sumela rests on legend: tradition names two Athenian monks in the 4th century.

Legacy

The Marciana remains one of Europe’s most important collections of Greek manuscripts. Bessarion’s reconciling Platonism left a deep mark on Renaissance philosophy.
