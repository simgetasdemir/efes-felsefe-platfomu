Key ideas

  1. The educator of Hellas

    In Book X of the Republic, Plato mentions those who say Homer was the educator of Hellas: poetry shaped how people lived. Perseus 606e ↗

  2. A reckoning with poetry

    In the same passage Plato asks which poetry belongs in a good city: poetry's power makes it both precious and dangerous. Perseus 606e ↗

  3. Myth and history

    UNESCO counts Schliemann's excavations, begun in 1870, as the starting point of modern archaeology: Troy is where a legend was tested with a spade. UNESCO ↗

Listen

Settlement at Troy goes back more than four thousand years; the excavations uncovered nine cities, one on top of another. Heinrich Schliemann began digging in 1870, and UNESCO counts his excavations as the starting point of modern archaeology. Homer's Iliad tells of warriors from Greece besieging this city, and it has inspired artists ever since. It matters for philosophy too: in the Republic, Plato mentions those who say Homer was the educator of Hellas, and questions the place of poetry in a good city. Stand in front of the walls and ask yourself: can a story teach us how to live?

Think

Which story has shaped you most? Does a story have to tell the truth?

What we don't know

How far the war of the Iliad rests on a historical event, and which layer is Homer's Troy, are still debated; who Homer was is not known for certain either.

Legacy

According to UNESCO, through Homer's Iliad and Virgil's Aeneid, Troy has had a profound influence on the arts for more than two millennia.
