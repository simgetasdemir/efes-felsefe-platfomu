Walk through Hacıbektaş

Hacı Bektaş Velî · 13th c. · Nevşehir

Hacı Bektaş Velî (c. 1209–1271) came to Anatolia from Khorasan and lived and taught here, at the place then called Sulucakarahöyük. The Bektashi order and Anatolian Alevism count him among their founding teachers; his lodge is now a museum.

The courtyards of the lodge

A lodge of three courtyards: the carved gate, the Fountain of the Three and the kitchen.

IdeaFour gates, forty stations

The Makâlât describes the path as four gates: the law (şeriat), the way (tarikat), knowledge of God (marifet) and reality (hakikat), each with ten stations. The seeker moves from outward rule to inward truth; for the Bektashis the inner meaning (bâtın) outweighs the outer form (zâhir). Wikipedia ↗

The Hall of the Forty

The wood-ceilinged hall where the cem gathering was held.

IdeaMaster of your hand, loins and tongue

The best known Bektashi maxim asks you to be master of three things: your hand (do not steal or harm), your loins (do not betray) and your tongue (do not lie or wound). Mystical teaching becomes an everyday ethics of restraint. Wikipedia ↗

IdeaSeventy-two peoples, one eye

A central Bektashi tenet is to look on all peoples and faiths with the same eye. Together with the cem, where women and men take part side by side, it gave the tradition its reputation for tolerance. Wikipedia ↗

Before you leave

Sit for a moment

Hacı Bektaş Velî was one of the dervishes who came west in the thirteenth century, in the years when the Mongols laid waste to Khorasan. He settled in this small village of central Anatolia, and nomad Turkmen, farmers and craftsmen gathered around him. The path that bears his name leads a person through four gates, from outward rule to inner truth. Yet its best known counsel is very plain: be master of your hand, your loins and your tongue. In the Hall of the Forty, women and men sat together, turned in the semah and sang hymns to the saz. Standing in the courtyard, ask yourself: does a person’s goodness show in great words, or in the everyday measure of hand and tongue?

Think

How well were you master of your hand, your loins and your tongue today?
