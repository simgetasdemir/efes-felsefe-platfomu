Walk through Lycia: Xanthos and Patara

The Lycian League; Montesquieu and the Federalist Papers · 4th c. BC – Roman period · Antalya, Muğla

The cities of Lycia formed a league with a common council: large cities had three votes, middling ones two, small towns one. Montesquieu chose Lycia as the model of an excellent confederate republic; the American Federalist Papers cited it too.

“Were I to give a model of an excellent confederate republic, I should pitch upon that of Lycia.”Montesquieu, The Spirit of the Laws IX.3

Before you leave

Sit for a moment

The cities of Lycia lived for centuries as a league. In the common council, large cities had three votes, middling ones two and small towns one; the council also appointed judges and magistrates. In the 18th century Montesquieu, in The Spirit of the Laws, held up Lycia as the model of an excellent confederate republic. In 1787 Alexander Hamilton quoted him in Federalist No. 9 and brought Lycia into the debate over the American constitution. Sit on the steps of the assembly hall at Patara: here small cities looked for a way to decide together with large ones without being crushed by them.

In a shared decision, how can the large and the small be fairly represented?
