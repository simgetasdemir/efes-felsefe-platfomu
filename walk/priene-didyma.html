Walk through Priene and Didyma

Bias of Priene; the oracle of Didyma · 6th–2nd c. BC · Aydın

Priene, the city of Bias, one of the Seven Sages, was refounded on its present site around 350 BC on a grid plan. Nearby Didyma held Apollo's oracle; there the Milesians began a temple meant to be the largest in the Greek world, and never finished it.

Before you leave

Sit for a moment

Bias was one of the Seven Sages of ancient tradition; Plato lists him alongside Thales. His city, Priene, first stood at the mouth of the Maeander; around 350 BC its people moved it to its present site at the foot of a mountain and laid it out on a grid of streets at right angles. Alexander the Great dedicated its main temple in 334 BC; the dedication inscription is now in the British Museum. Nearby Didyma held the oracle of Apollo. There the Milesians began a temple meant to be the largest in the Greek world, but never finished it. As you walk among its unfinished columns, ask: is it beginning a work that matters, or finishing it?

Think

Would you begin a work you knew you could never finish?
