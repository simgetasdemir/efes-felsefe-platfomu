Walk through Trabzon and Sumela

Bessarion of Trebizond; the scholars of Trebizond · 7th–15th c. · Trabzon

Born in Trebizond, Bessarion (1403–1472) became the Renaissance thinker who tried to reconcile Plato and Aristotle; his library is the core of the Biblioteca Marciana in Venice. The Empire of Trebizond was a centre of Byzantine science, and the Sumela Monastery, clinging to its cliff, is an emblem of withdrawal into contemplation.

IdeaPlato or Aristotle?

In the fifteenth century Byzantium and Italy argued over whether Plato or Aristotle stood higher. Bessarion answered George of Trebizond’s attack on Plato with In calumniatorem Platonis: he defended Plato as the philosopher closest to Christian thought, yet tried to reconcile the two without belittling Aristotle. IdeaGiving away a library

After the fall of Constantinople Bessarion made it his task to save Greek manuscripts, and in 1468 he gave the hundreds he had gathered to Venice. The gift became the core of the Biblioteca Marciana; it was largely in this way that Greek thought reached Renaissance Europe. SEP ↗

Before you leave

Sit for a moment

You reach Sumela by climbing through the forest of the Altındere valley. The monastery clings so closely to the cliff that from afar it looks like part of the rock. For centuries monks withdrew here from the world to pray, read and cover the walls with paintings. Trebizond below was the opposite: a crowded port where the Silk Road came down to the Black Sea. Bessarion, born in that city, became both a monk and a man of the world: he defended Plato, rescued manuscripts and gave his books to a city. Looking down into the valley from the monastery, ask: does thinking need withdrawal from the world, or a life within it?
